# Sieve animation: test results (workstream C, 2026-11-13)

- **Unit tests** (`common/src/test`, run in every module): keyframe tracks (linear, step, catmull-rom, bezier hard stop, clamping), easing and spring, bone matrices (rotation about a pivot, parents, position and scale), the JSON loader (rejects an expression keyframe, an unknown interpolation, a missing parent or texture, a missing contract bone), the sieve animator (load plop and event, shake clip and fall back to idle, finds reveal and pop, finish and hop, far level stays at rest, arrays reused), and the shipped `sieve.json` (contract bones, all 8 clips, shake swing inside the frame opening, 13 cubes in moving bones). 29 tests (5 more from the scaffold).
- **Headless self test** (`python scripts/server_check.py all`): PASS on neoforge-26.2 (132 checks), forge-26.2 (127), neoforge-26.3 (132), forge-26.3 (127). The workstream C checks: sieve state properties and every state placeable, a block entity that implements `SieveViewSource`, particle types registered and their files present, `sieve.json` loads, every blockstate and model parses with elements inside the block, sounds.json entries and all 27 files present, selection shapes inside the block.
- **Real client** (`python scripts/client_tour.py <layer>`, isolated launcher, 17 frames each): PASS on neoforge-26.2 and forge-26.2. Frames in `docs/frames/primamateria_<layer>_*.png`: sieve at rest from two sides, each of the five mesh tiers, loaded and waiting, two frames mid-shake (plus two more), a thin layer with finds showing, a full load of sand, 20 sieves moving, and the same 20 with `client.animations` reduced and off.
- **Cost with 20 moving sieves in view** (client tour, uncapped frame rate): our renderer code takes 5.5 microseconds per sieve per frame on neoforge-26.2 and 4.7 on forge-26.2, that is about 0.1 ms per frame for 20 sieves; with `reduced` or `off` it takes 2.4 to 3.0 microseconds per sieve. Average frame rate in `full`: 206 (neoforge-26.2) and 204 (forge-26.2) with the rest of the scene. The `reduced` and `off` runs of the same tour reported about 30 fps on both builds, which is the isolated desktop slowing the client after the first measurement, not the renderer (its own time went down), so there is no real frame rate comparison between the three levels yet.
- **Not covered:** the 26.3 client tour (the 26.3 builds are covered by the headless self test only; the tour needs no 26.3-specific code), the 200-sieve hall of `SIFTING.md` (20 were measured), sounds heard in a real game (the launcher mutes the client), the powered drive and auto hammer in a real client.

## Round 2 (branch workstream-c2)

- **Top texture from the block model.** `SieveModels.fromModel` reads the up face of the loaded block's baked model (`ModelManager.getBlockStateModelSet`, `BlockStateModel.collectParts`, `BlockStateModelPart.getQuads(UP)`, `BakedQuad.materialInfo()`), skips animated quads, and uses the sprite's own texture file; a tint index on the quad tints the layer. The file-name guess (`_top`, plain, `_side`) is the fallback, then a neutral sprite with one log line. Frame `sieve_materials_*` (neoforge-26.2 and forge-26.2): red sand, soul sand, hay bale, bookshelf (its top is oak planks, which a file-name guess cannot find) and grass block (tinted green). I could not register a test-pack block in the dev environment; the vanilla bookshelf is the case of a top texture not named like the block.
- **Machine renderer** (`MachineRenderer`, `QuadBuffer`, `MachineAssets`): wooden and clay barrel (fluid or compost surface at its level in the view's color, still strip of animated fluid textures, growing crystals, soak item sinking, finished item), crucible (melt or fluid surface at its level, glowing, solid item on top, pour streams from the lips), casting tray (melt level, color cooling to gray, cast item), windup drive (key turns back with the stored turns), furnace drive (pulsing fire behind the grate), powered drive (charge strip segments, glowing), auto hammer (two stamps, alternate strokes, block under the stamps). One reusable buffer per texture, rebuilt each frame from a handful of boxes; nothing is drawn for an empty machine; beyond half of `client.animation_distance` only the surface is drawn; `client.animations=off` draws the surface only. Frames `machines_barrels`, `machines_crucible_tray`, `machines_drives`, `machines_drives_b` on both loaders. Crystals sit below a high fluid level (800 mB covers them), and the crystal textures `crystal_*.png` are not drawn yet, so the vanilla amethyst cluster stands in.
- **Fill tint** (`FillTint`, registered in both client classes): the still layer of the `fill` models takes the average color of the loaded block's top texture. Frame `sieve_off_fill_tint`: soul sand brown, red sand orange. The chunk only re-meshes when the block state changes, so the block entity must have the load before `FILL` changes.
- **26.3 client tour:** passes on neoforge-26.3 (24 frames in `docs/frames/primamateria_neoforge-26.3_*`).
- **Crystal model:** `branding/models/crystal.bbmodel`, `models/block/crystal.json`; the renderer draws the same three prisms scaled 0.2 to 1.0.

### Exact edits to shared files for the lead (this branch, on top of workstream-c)

- `ModContent.makeBlock`: before `return new Block(props);` add `if (isMachine(name)) { return new com.itmecasey.primamateria.block.MachinePreviewBlock(props, shapeOf(name)); }`, plus the two private helper methods `isMachine` and `shapeOf` added below it. Replace this with A's real barrel, crucible, tray, drive and auto hammer blocks; each real block entity implements `anim.MachineViewSource` (`machineView()` returns the view record of its kind) and the renderer is registered for its block entity type as `MachineRenderer<YourBlockEntity>::new`. The renderer chooses what to draw from the block's registry name (`wooden_barrel`, `clay_barrel`, `crucible`, `casting_tray`, `windup_drive`, `furnace_drive`, `powered_drive`, `auto_hammer`).
- `NeoForgeClient` and `ForgeClient` (26.2 and 26.3): a second `registerBlockEntityRenderer(ModAnim.MACHINE_PREVIEW, MachineRenderer<...>::new)` line and a `RegisterColorHandlersEvent` listener (`BlockTintSources` on NeoForge, `Block` on Forge) that registers `FillTint` for the sieve block.
- `ModAnim` (mine): the `MACHINE_PREVIEW` block entity type and its list of block names; `registerBlockEntityTypes` now also registers `machine_preview`.
- `sync_versions.py`: `patch_preview_blocks` (26.3 has no block codec) covers both preview blocks.
- Models: the windup key and the auto hammer's stamps are no longer in the static models; the renderer draws them. A's blocks must therefore have the machine renderer, or those parts are missing.
- For A: a barrel, crucible or tray view must change when its contents change (the renderer reads the view every frame); the sieve's block entity must carry the load before `FILL` changes (the tint is read at re-mesh).
